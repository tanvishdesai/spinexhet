Abstract

012
013

Post-hoc visual explanation methods are widely used to justify image classifiers, yet
different methods can highlight contradictory regions for the same prediction. This disagreement is especially risky in clinical vision, where saliency maps may create unwarranted confidence in automated decisions. We study whether such disagreement is merely
method-specific noise or whether it is systematically shaped by model architecture. We
introduce S PINE XN ET-B ENCH, a two-domain benchmark spanning RSNA lumbar spine
magnetic resonance imaging and CUB-200 fine-grained bird classification, with eight
architectures, seven explanation methods, five-fold validation, expert-coordinate clinical alignment, model-randomization checks, and consensus explanation variants. Across
both domains, explanation agreement follows a stable three-tier pattern: classic convolutional networks agree most strongly, modern convolutional networks form a domainsensitive middle tier, and patch-based Transformers agree least. On RSNA, DenseNet121 and ResNet-50 reach mean pairwise Spearman agreement of 0.434 and 0.412, while
ViT-Small and DeiT-Small remain near 0.200 and 0.203 despite competitive classification AUCs. The pattern reproduces on CUB-200, where DenseNet-121 reaches 0.518
and DeiT-Small remains lowest at 0.220; the cross-domain architecture-rank correlation across the six shared architectures is ρs = 0.886 (p = 0.019). Consensus helps:
faithfulness-weighted consensus outperforms uniform averaging in the current repaired
RSNA ablation, and an oracle top-k variant estimates the upper-bound benefit of method
curation. [REPAIR PLACEHOLDER: replace consensus sentence after RSNA and
CUB repair notebooks finish] Finally, expert-coordinate overlap remains below 0.28
IoU for post-hoc maps, while a controlled concept bottleneck model corrects 59.2% of
previously wrong RSNA predictions through concept intervention. These results argue
that explanation reliability is not solely a property of the XAI method; it is constrained
by the inductive bias of the underlying architecture.

Related Work

118

Post-hoc visual explanations. Saliency methods estimate which input regions support a
prediction. Gradient-based saliency traces derivatives with respect to pixels [15]; Integrated
120
Gradients accumulates gradients from a baseline to the input and satisfies attribution ax121
ioms [16]; and GradientSHAP estimates SHAP-style attributions through noisy gradient
122 sampling. Perturbation approaches such as occlusion remove or reveal image regions and
123 measure the output change [20]. Class activation mapping methods, including Grad-CAM
124 and Grad-CAM++, localize class evidence through convolutional feature maps [4, 14]. For
125 Transformers, Attention Rollout aggregates attention flow through layers [1]. These meth126 ods are often treated as interchangeable explanations of a fixed prediction, but their compu127 tational assumptions differ substantially.
119

128
129

Explanation disagreement and benchmark reliability. Krishna et al. formalized explanation disagreement and showed that practitioners lack principled ways to resolve conflicting
131
explanations [11]. Adebayo et al. demonstrated that some saliency maps can fail model132
parameter sanity checks [2]. Recent large-scale XAI benchmarks such as LATEC evaluate
133 explanation methods and metrics across broad method-metric grids [8]. Our focus is or134 thogonal: we ask whether inter-method explanation disagreement is shaped by architecture
135 class and whether the resulting hierarchy reproduces across clinical and natural image do136 mains. Thus, while LATEC studies metric reliability at scale, our work studies architecture137 conditioned disagreement, cross-domain stability, and expert-coordinate alignment.
130

Architecture and inductive bias. Modern vision models differ in spatial inductive bias. 138
ResNet and DenseNet rely on convolutional locality and hierarchical feature reuse [7, 9]. 139
EfficientNet uses compound scaling of depth, width, and resolution [17], while ConvNeXt 140
modernizes convolutional design choices in response to Transformer-era architectures [12]. 141
ViT and DeiT tokenize images into patches and use self-attention rather than convolutional 142
spatial aggregation [5, 18]. These architectural differences motivate our central question: do 143
explanation methods disagree differently when the classifier computation itself changes?
144
Concurrent work on hypothesis-class effects reaches a related conclusion from the oppo- 145
site experimental direction [3]. That work fixes the attribution setting and varies prediction- 146
equivalent model classes, showing that models from the same hypothesis class produce more 147
consistent feature attributions than cross-class model pairs. Our study fixes each trained vi- 148
sion model and varies the explanation method, then tests whether the resulting inter-method 149
disagreement hierarchy reproduces across clinical MRI and fine-grained natural images. The 150
overlap is therefore the broad principle that model class is not explanation-neutral; the dif- 151
ference is that we study within-model method disagreement, cross-domain visual evidence, 152
and clinical expert-coordinate alignment.
153
154
155

Concept bottlenecks and clinical XAI. Concept bottleneck models (CBMs) predict humaninterpretable concepts before making a final prediction and support intervention by editing 156
concept values [10]. In health care, however, post-hoc explanations can create a false sense 157
of interpretability if they do not correspond to clinically meaningful evidence [6]. We there- 158
fore treat the CBM in this paper as a controlled ante-hoc alternative rather than simply an- 159
other saliency method. Because our concepts are deterministic and metadata-derived, this 160
choice isolates architecture and explanation-method effects without adding a noisy concept- 161
162
discovery problem.

218

Clinical Alignment and Concept Intervention

Expert-coordinate alignment on RSNA remains low for all post-hoc explanations. The best
mean consensus expert IoU is DenseNet-121 at 0.275 ± 0.002, followed by ResNet-50 at
402
0.265 ± 0.008 and the non-leaky CBM at 0.264 ± 0.017. DeiT-Small reaches 0.255 ± 0.005,
403
while ViT-Small is lower at 0.210 ± 0.013. The correct interpretation is not that post-hoc
404
maps are clinically aligned, but that even the best average overlap with expert-localized
405 pathology remains below 30%.
406
If disagreement is intrinsic to post-hoc explanations for some architecture classes, ante407
hoc interpretability becomes attractive not merely for transparency but for stability and in408
tervention. The concept bottleneck model offers this different interaction mode. On 1,000
409 RSNA validation samples, the non-leaky CBM is initially correct on 848 samples. Of the
410 152 wrong predictions, editing one concept can correct 90, yielding a 59.2% fix rate (Ta411 ble 4). The most useful concept is adjacent pathology density, which alone fixes 51.3% of
412 the wrong cases. The leaky CBM achieves a higher intervention fix rate, but its dominant
413 concept encodes pathology presence and is therefore treated only as an ablation.

ence did not explain the full architecture hierarchy: the correlation between feature coher- 417
ence and agreement is weak across all architectures, though it matches the CNN ordering
418
more closely. This suggests that Transformer disagreement is not captured by smoothness of
419
intermediate features alone. Second, EfficientNet-B4 remains a domain-sensitive middle-tier
420
architecture rather than a failed run: an audit of the initially suspicious Integrated Gradients
421
and GradientSHAP scores confirmed non-identical maps across all folds, with details moved
422
to the supplementary material.

5

Discussion

425
426

The central lesson is that explanation reliability is not solely a property of the XAI method; 427
it is constrained by the inductive bias of the underlying architecture. The claim is empirical, 428
not causal proof: we do not show that architecture alone determines saliency. However, 429
across two domains, multiple folds, and independently trained backbones, architecture class 430
is a strong organizing factor for inter-method agreement. This has practical consequences. A 431
high-performing classifier can still be a poor candidate for post-hoc explanation if its saliency 432
433
methods disagree severely.
The Transformer result is the most stable family-level finding. ViT-Small and DeiT- 434
Small both occupy the low-agreement tier on RSNA and CUB-200. DeiT’s low agreement 435
is especially informative because distillation from a convolutional teacher does not make its 436
explanations CNN-like. A plausible mechanism is that patch tokenization weakens the local 437
spatial continuity that CAM and gradient methods exploit, while self-attention creates non- 438
local evidence pathways that different attribution families interrogate differently. In contrast, 439
DenseNet and ResNet preserve stronger convolutional locality and hierarchical feature reuse, 440
which may stabilize where multiple attribution methods place evidence. Attention Rollout 441
partly addresses this by using architecture-native attention flow, but the broader inter-method 442
disagreement remains.
443
The domain-sensitive middle tier is also informative. EfficientNet shifts from low agree- 444
ment on RSNA to a stronger middle position on CUB-200, and ConvNeXt similarly im- 445
proves on CUB. This rank movement is consistent with the three-tier claim rather than a 446
contradiction: compound scaling and modern convolutional design may behave differently
447
on small, low-contrast MRI crops than on rich-texture natural images. The result cautions
448
against strict architecture rankings while supporting architecture class as an organizing fac449
tor.
450
The consensus experiments show that resolving disagreement is not as simple as aver451
aging every map. Faithfulness-weighted consensus is a principled baseline, but oracle top-k
consensus performs better in the current ablation set. This suggests a general deployment 452
rule: when explanations disagree, first identify methods that are faithful for the model and 453
domain on validation data, then combine only that curated subset. The oracle result should 454
455
be read as evidence that curation matters, not as a finished inference-time protocol.
The clinical results argue for caution. Post-hoc saliency maps overlap expert-coordinate 456
regions less than 30% of the time on average. This is not enough to support uncritical clinical 457
deployment. The CBM result is useful because it shifts the user interaction from viewing a 458
heatmap to editing a concept and observing the prediction change. Still, because our CBM 459

7

Conclusion

478

Post-hoc explanation disagreement is not merely a property of explanation methods in isolation. In our two-domain benchmark, architecture class strongly structures whether those
481 methods agree. Classic convolutional networks produce comparatively stable explanations,
482 modern convolutional networks are domain-sensitive, and patch-based Transformers show
483 consistently low agreement even when classification performance is competitive. Consen484 sus can reduce the burden of choosing a single map, but the oracle top-k ablation shows
485 that curation is more promising than unfiltered averaging. In safety-critical settings, low
486 expert-coordinate overlap makes ante-hoc and intervention-based interpretability especially
487 important. Architecture-aware explanation evaluation should therefore become part of model
selection, not an afterthought applied after accuracy has already chosen the model.
